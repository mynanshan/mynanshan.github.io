About

I am a PhD candidate in Statistics at Simon Fraser University. My research focuses on statistical methodology for longitudinal and high-dimensional data, particularly when observations are sparse, irregular, and structurally complex.

I am especially interested in functional data analysis, neuroimaging, survival analysis, and statistical machine learning. I aim to develop methods that are both theoretically principled and useful for scientific applications.

Projects

Functional data · Online learning

OnlineFPCA

Online functional principal component analysis for streaming multidimensional data. We combine spline representations with Riemannian stochastic gradient descent to learn smooth, orthonormal components, and use beam search with rolling validation to tune smoothing as new data arrive.

Scientific computing · Medical imaging

TumorPDE

A research codebase for simulating and reconstructing glioma progression on patient-specific 3D brain domains. A reaction–diffusion model describes tumor infiltration; sensitivity equations support calibration of growth, diffusion, and tumor origin from single or multiple scans.

Survival analysis · Neuroimaging

Survival modeling with image covariates

Our SMMR work uses a regularized mixture cure-rate model to distinguish susceptibility to Alzheimer’s conversion from time to conversion, with localized MRI effects estimated over irregular brain domains.

In ongoing work, we jointly model longitudinal images and interval-censored event times through functional principal components linked to a Cox model, enabling efficient estimation and inference for the overall image effect.

Dynamical systems · Sparse estimation

Differential equation models

Two papers on learning sparse dynamical systems from noisy, non-Gaussian observations. We develop estimation and structure-selection methods for high-dimensional linear ODEs and a joint estimation approach for sparse additive ODEs, with applications including yeast gene regulation.
