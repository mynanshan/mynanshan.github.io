About

I am a PhD candidate in Statistics at Simon Fraser University. My research focuses on statistical methodology for longitudinal and high-dimensional data, particularly when observations are sparse, irregular, and structurally complex.

I am especially interested in functional data analysis, longitudinal neuroimaging, survival analysis, structured covariance models, and statistical machine learning. I aim to develop methods that are both theoretically principled and useful for scientific applications.

Research

My current work includes functional principal component methods for sparsely observed matrix-valued trajectories and joint models connecting longitudinal neuroimaging with time-to-event outcomes. I am also exploring population-informed models for subject-specific dynamics and evolving geometry.
